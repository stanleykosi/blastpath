## Event decision brief

Three track winners become the only overall finalists. Published unweighted criteria are technical execution, graph-native HydraDB use, completeness/usability, result quality, and originality. A valid submission needs the official form, public repository with open-source license/setup/attribution, and an accessible demo video at most three minutes. Meaningful HydraDB use and post-August-12 participant work are mandatory.

## Why this concept

BlastPath directly answers the Track 2A prompt with a buildable two-day workflow. Unlike a generic SCA scanner, it combines cross-repository dependency paths with incident-window build evidence, explicit uncertainty, shared-path containment ranking, and a visible before/after simulation. The real May 2026 TanStack event supplies a recent six-minute incident narrative.

## Essential technical evidence

HydraDB provides JSON HTTP and Bolt APIs, numeric node IDs, scalar properties, parameterized `UNWIND`, bounded variable-length traversal, and native `SPpaths`, `SSpaths`, and `MSpaths`. Current caveats support HTTP-first, bounded/simple patterns, version pinning, deterministic reseed, and early Phase-B smoke proof.

## Scope conclusion

Build one polished npm-v3 incident workflow using three synthetic services and a normalized slice of a real advisory. Do not attempt ecosystem-scale crawling, multiple package managers, live OAuth, maintainer/typosquat analysis, or an LLM. Winning is not guaranteed; a vulnerability table plus force graph is unlikely to differentiate. Temporal evidence, exact HydraDB execution, and containment replay are required.
