# Official Hack Hydra requirements

Verified from the official event page on 2026-08-18. Deadline: **2026-08-20 11:59 PM America/Los_Angeles (PDT, UTC-7)**, equal to **2026-08-21 06:59 UTC / 07:59 Africa/Lagos**.

## Eligibility and work rules

- Online, free, global language; team size 1–4.
- One person may join only one team or participate solo.
- Submitted project work must start on or after August 12, 2026. Judges may inspect participant commit history.
- Existing open-source libraries, frameworks, APIs, datasets, general infrastructure, models, and AI coding assistants are allowed.
- The project must be original and substantially created during the event.
- Attribute third-party code, APIs, libraries, datasets, and templates.

## Track selected

Track 2A: Supply chain blast radius. The prompt asks entrants to build an npm or PyPI dependency graph and answer transitive organizational exposure, compromised-version timing, lockfile resolution during the malicious window, shared maintainer/infrastructure links, typosquat proximity, and complete blast radius. BlastPath deliberately solves the core organizational exposure, timing, and containment workflow; maintainer/infrastructure and typosquat features are documented post-hackathon scope.

## Required submission

All three must be complete by the deadline:

1. Official submission form.
2. Public GitHub repository accessible without permission.
3. Working pitch/demo video no longer than 3:00; content after 3:00 may not be reviewed.

Repository must include complete source, open-source license, clear README, setup/run instructions, HydraDB explanation, environment/dependency requirements, and third-party attribution. Video must state the problem, explain what was built, show it working, and explain where and why HydraDB is used.

## Prize and judging structure

- $5,000 Grand Champion; $3,000 runner-up; $1,500 third; $500 Best Use of HydraDB.
- Best entry in each of three tracks advances; those finalists are ranked holistically.
- Published unweighted criteria: technical execution, graph-native HydraDB use, completeness/usability, quality, originality.
- Strong entries have a functional demo, real ingestion/retrieval, a clear use case, and thoughtful implementation.

## Disqualification guardrails

No pre-August-12 participant work, private/missing repo, missing license, missing video, superficial HydraDB use, late submission, or rule/code-of-conduct violation. Test repository, video, demo, and form links before submission.

## Unknown; do not invent

Country/sanctions/age/tax/payment restrictions, ecosystem-scale expectations for Track 2A, preferred image/commit, and unpublished tie-breakers were not stated. Ask organizers in Discord when practical.
